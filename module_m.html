Proposal Narrative Rules

The structural and stylistic rules governing every client-facing sentence Create The Edge writes. These are not guidelines; they are the standard against which every proposal is validated before it leaves the office.

Two-Paragraph Rule

Paragraph 1 — Context (Why)
Identify the project's importance and the client's situation
ImpactWhat does completing this project accomplish?
Critical EventWhat happens if the timeline or budget is missed?
Emotional DecisionHow will the end-user feel when it is done?
Rational DecisionSquare footage, population, budget, menu preferences
Paragraph 2 — Scope (What)
Describe Create The Edge's exact design scope
Spaces designedList every space with exact SF (round to whole numbers)
Equipment approachKey equipment features and Create The Edge's design strategy
Client benefitHow Create The Edge's approach specifically benefits this client
✓ Write This
  • "This renovation will modernize kitchen and laundry infrastructure to support projected occupancy increases while minimizing long-term capital, labor, and utility costs."
  • "The 1,167 SF kitchen serving 490 residents will require a design that balances security, operational efficiency, and long-term durability."
  • State abbreviations: GA, NC, FL, AL
  • Round numbers: 438 SF, not 437.86 SF
✗ Never Write This
  • "We are pleased to present this proposal."
  • "We hope to meet your expectations."
  • "We look forward to the opportunity."
  • "Emotionally, the client wants..."
  • "Rationally, the decision is driven by..."
  • Em dashes anywhere in any document
  • Full state names: [State], [State]

Vendor-Neutral Paragraph Variants

Standard Letter Templates

Four standard legal documents used in every Create The Edge client engagement. Templates live in the standard templates folder; update the project name and date before every use. Never send without review.

Standard Document
Consultation Agreement
Binding contract included as final pages of every fee proposal. Key terms: 7-day termination notice; net-30 payment; reactivation fee after 4-month suspension; no work before signature. Supersedes all prior negotiations. Article II sets the maximum fee and phase breakdown.

Issued when client requires insurance coverage beyond Create The Edge's policy. Describes limited scope (drawings only; no products, no installation; 2–3 site visits per project). M.9 · Operational Reference

Pre-Flight Checklist

The mandatory quality check before any proposal leaves the office. Every box must be confirmed. A proposal sent without completing this checklist is a proposal sent at risk.

Content & Scope

  • Sector-specific independence paragraph correct (F&B, Foodservice, Correctional, Education, Healthcare, or Religious variant as appropriate)
  • Square footage stated for every designed area; rounded to whole numbers; matches the FTE/bed/key count and equipment budget
  • Two-paragraph intro only (context + scope); no marketing fluff; no framework labels (emotional, rational, critically) in client text
  • Letter of Objective included for all RFP responses (not required for RFQ)
  • Phase titles accurate (SD/DD/CD/BN/CA or variant format appropriate for this project type)
  • Meeting counts confirmed and stated explicitly; additional meetings billed at $225/hr language included
  • All spaces listed in the cover letter and in the Whereas clause of the Consultation Agreement
  • MasterSpec and MEP loads reviewed for coordination accuracy

Format & Compliance

  • Travel line items separated from base fee; $1,600/day + $0.665/mile beyond 50 miles from [City, State]
  • Insurance limits stated ($2M/$5M Professional; $2M/$4M General Liability)
  • "Create The Edge" lowercase, bold, #266889 throughout; never capitalized as a brand name
  • No em dashes anywhere in the document; use commas, semicolons, or full sentences instead
  • US state abbreviations only (GA, NC, FL, AL); never full state names in formal text
  • No juvenile phrasing ("we are pleased," "we hope," "we look forward," "thank you for the opportunity")
  • Round numbers in narrative (438 SF, not 437.86 SF)
  • Date and contact info current and correct; no stale dates from prior proposals

Risk Flags & Final Steps

  • Flags raised for any scope risks, undersized areas, or code conditions identified during document review
  • Variance letter attached if client requires coverage beyond current policy limits
  • Litigation letter attached if required by the RFQ/RFP solicitation documents
  • SF 330 selected using the bullseye method; five projects most relevant to this sector/architect/geography
  • Filed in [CRM Software] with two-paragraph narrative saved in the project record
  • Filed Year-Month-XX naming convention in Box
  • Recipient email verified against the original RFP or inquiry email before sending
