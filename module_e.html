E.1 · Why Discovery Comes First

Why Discovery Comes Before Everything

Discovery is the intelligence phase of proposal development. It begins the moment a request arrives and ends when you have enough verified information to calculate the fee and write the scope with confidence.

Every cost overrun, every scope dispute, every project that blew past its allotted hours shares a common root cause: a proposal written before the writer fully understood the project. Discovery is not a courtesy step. It is the foundation every downstream decision rests on, from fee calculation to the exact language in the consultation agreement.

At [Company Name], discovery begins the moment the request arrives. Whether it comes as an RFP, an RFQ, a forwarded email from [Company Owner], or a direct architect call, the clock starts. The first job is not to write, not to calculate, and not to call. The first job is to read and understand.

❞ Expert Insight
"When the data you need does not exist in the documents you receive, you go get it. Measure it yourself. Walk the site. Confirm the project scope area firsthand. The consultant who relies only on what was handed to them will always be less accurate than the one who verified it."
[Training Program] · 40 Years in the Room

The real cost of skipping discovery: a university child development center project where the kitchen grew from 400 square feet to 1,200 square feet after the proposal was already written and accepted. The project team spent 80 hours in conceptual design alone, well beyond the 60 to 70 hours budgeted for the entire engagement. The client resisted paying the overage. Root cause: the proposal was written on an estimate, not verified information.

No proposal goes to [Department Manager] for review until all five discovery data points are either confirmed or documented as missing with a benchmark figure substituted. A proposal built on unverified assumptions is not a proposal. It is a liability.

E.2 · Reading the RFP and Drawings First

Reading the RFP and Drawings First

Before you make a call, before you open a template, before you calculate a fee: read everything the architect or client has sent. Many requests contain all the information you need. Calling without reading first signals you did not review their submission.
Figure E.2.1 · What to Hunt for When Reading Every Request Document
What to FindWhy It Matters
Project Type & SectorSets the sector rate, template, and fee formula
Square Footage / Kitchen SizeStated or measurable via measurement software; primary fee calculation input
Submission DeadlineDeliver before deadline, not at it
RFP vs. RFQ SignalFee request = RFP. Qualifications only = RFQ
Submittal RequirementsForms and attachments required; missing one = automatic disqualification
Population DriverFTE, beds, end users, headcount; drives kitchen sizing
Scope SignalsFull prep, warming only, bar, coffee program included?
Operational RequirementsMeals, shifts, service style

When project documents are attached, use measurement software to measure the kitchen footprint directly from the plans. This is the most accurate method when project scope area is not stated. Always note whether project scope area came from a stated figure or a digital measurement tool, because if the project scope changes later, that documentation is your basis for an additional service request.

⚠ Watch Out
Do not call the architect to ask for information already in the documents they sent. This signals you did not read the request. It damages credibility before the relationship has begun. Read everything first. Call only if genuinely necessary, and only with a prepared question list.
☰ From the Files: Square Footage Discovery in Practice
A institutional project proposal arrived with project documents showing 4,001 square feet. The verified end user count was 490. Applying the benchmark formula (10 sq ft per end user for populations under 500) produced a calculated need of 4,900 square feet. The discrepancy was documented in the proposal as a discovery note. [Company Name] designed to the drawing dimensions while flagging the population-based calculation, protecting the firm if the project expanded.

E.3 · The One Prepared Call

The One Prepared Call

Once you have read all available documents and identified what remains unclear, you are ready to call, if a call is necessary. The governing principle at [Company Name] is the One Prepared Call: contact the architect or client once, with all questions consolidated and listed in advance.

You do not call twice because you thought of something else. Multiple calls from the same firm on the same project signal disorganization. Architects work with dozens of consultants; the ones they remember are the ones who are efficient on first contact.

▦ Standard Acknowledgment Format
"Thank you for inviting [Company Name] to partner with [Firm Name] on [Project Name]. We appreciate the opportunity and will review your information. If we have any questions, we will give you a call."

Send this immediately on receipt, before reading anything. It confirms arrival, signals professionalism, and sets the expectation that any call will be purposeful and prepared.

✓ Best Practice: Rapport on the Call

Whether gathered through documents or a call, the fee calculation cannot begin until these five data points are confirmed or replaced with a documented benchmark. A proposal that goes to review without all five addressed will come back.
1
Confirmation Point
Project Type & Sector
K-12, Healthcare, Correctional, Corporate, other. Drives the template, rate, and formula used for the entire proposal.
2

3

4

5
Confirmation Point

Date and time, delivery method, copies required, required attachments. Deliver before the deadline, not at it. Anchors the three business day internal turnaround target.

⚠ Watch Out: Coffee Programs
A "coffee bar" is not a simple add-on. A full specialty service program starts at $80,000 to $150,000, with an espresso machine alone costing $35,000 to $45,000. If the client mentions a coffee bar in passing, clarify immediately: grab-and-go or full espresso service? The scope and fee difference is enormous.

E.5 · The Questionnaire System

The Questionnaire System

For complex projects where a single call cannot gather all required information, [Company Name] uses questionnaires: structured documents sent to the architect or client. They are a supplement for projects with too many variables to resolve verbally, not a replacement for the One Prepared Call.

The governing principle is precision. Vague questions produce vague answers. Vague answers produce assumptions. Assumptions produce errors in the proposal. Every questionnaire question must be specific enough that the recipient can answer it with a concrete fact, number, or decision.

❞ On Questionnaire Precision
"The things that I've learned are to be able to ask more precise, direct questions. Otherwise you get vague information, and then you put yourself into it, and you go, 'Well, this is what I would do.' Whereas if you had the specific information, you could go, 'This is what we should do.'"
[Training Program] · The Proposal Standard
When to Use a Questionnaire
  • The project has multiple service areas with different operational requirements (executive dining plus cafeteria plus catering)
  • The request does not specify operational details and the documents do not answer them
  • The project involves specialty service line in addition to professional services, requiring separate question sets
  • The client is the owner directly rather than an architect, and may not know standard AEC terminology
⚠ Watch Out: Over-Burdening the Architect
A 40-question questionnaire sent to an architect on a routine school project signals poor judgment. Architects are managing multiple consultants on tight timelines. Use questionnaires for complex projects only. For straightforward requests, the One Prepared Call is sufficient.
▦ Rule of the House: Template Integrity
The master questionnaire template lives in [Cloud Storage] > Marketing Proposals > Proposal Templates. Copy it into the specific project folder before making any changes. Any edit made to the master template directly corrupts it for every future project. This rule has no exceptions.

☰ From the Files: The Shift Question That Changes the Fee
A corporate dining project stated 50 employees. A standard questionnaire might calculate for all 50 at once. The actual situation: three separate shifts, maximum 18 employees eating during any one period. Designing for 50 simultaneous diners would have over-specified the kitchen by nearly 200%. The shift question, asked precisely, reduced equipment cost and right-sized the fee to what the project actually required.

E.7 · When Information Is Missing

When Information Is Missing

Missing information is not a reason to stall. It is a situation with a defined response. At [Company Name], there are exactly three appropriate responses to missing discovery data, applied in order.
1
Response One
Request It
Ask the architect or client directly. Document the request and date in [CRM Software]. Frame it as a service: "To ensure our proposal reflects your actual project scope rather than a general estimate, we need to confirm [specific item]."
2
Response Two
Benchmark It
If the deadline is too tight, apply the appropriate benchmark and document it clearly. For K-12/institutional under 500: multiply population by 10 sq ft. The benchmark figure is always identified as such, never presented as a verified measurement.
3
Response Three
Flag It
When you have used a benchmark or estimate, include a discovery note in the proposal. This shows the client [Company Name] is being transparent about the data used, and creates the legal basis for an additional service request if the actual scope differs significantly.
Escalate
If All Three Fail
Do Not Proceed Alone
If information is fundamentally missing and no benchmark can responsibly substitute, escalate to [Department Manager] or [Company Owner]. Escalation is not failure; it is the correct professional response.
▦ Rule of the House: Document Every Assumption
Any figure in the fee calculation that is a benchmark rather than a verified measurement must be labeled as such. If the actual project scope area later differs from the estimate by more than 15%, that documented assumption is your basis for requesting an additional service fee. Without the documentation, you have no basis for the request.

✓ Best Practice: The One-Paragraph Discovery Summary
Before starting to write, take five minutes to write a single paragraph summarizing everything confirmed in discovery: project type, population, project scope area source, operational scope, and deadline. This summary becomes the first entry in your fee calculation notes. If you cannot write the paragraph without using the word "unknown," you are not ready to write the proposal.

E.10 · Saving & Organizing in [Cloud Storage]

Saving and Organizing in [Cloud Storage]

Discovery produces documents: the original request email, project documents, questionnaire responses, digital measurement tool exports, and fee calculation notes. All must be saved in the correct Box folder structure before the proposal goes to review.

The organization process follows the same sequence every time: [CRM Software] first, [Cloud Storage] second. Create the deal in [CRM Software] before creating the folder in [Cloud Storage]. The [CRM Software] deal number becomes part of the Box folder name, creating a linked reference trail that allows anyone on the team to find all project documents from either system.

▦ Rule of the House: [CRM Software] First, [Cloud Storage] Second
Create the [CRM Software] deal entry first. Use that deal number as the folder number in [Cloud Storage]. Then create the [Cloud Storage] folder. Never create the [Cloud Storage] folder without a corresponding [CRM Software] entry. The two systems are your linked audit trail and must stay synchronized.
☰ From the Files: The Template Copy Rule in Practice
Navigate to Marketing Proposals > Proposal Templates, copy the professional services or food & beverage template that matches the project type, paste it into the specific project folder, and rename it following the naming convention. The rule is absolute: you are always working in a copy inside the project folder. The template in the Templates folder is never edited directly.

15Shift Structure Question
