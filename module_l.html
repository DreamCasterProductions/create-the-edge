Alphabetical Entries

A – C

From addendum to construction administration, these entries cover the foundational document types, approval workflows, and client-facing terminology that every Create The Edge associate must command from day one.
A
Addendum
Proposal
A formal written modification issued during the bidding phase (Phase IV) that clarifies, corrects, or supplements the construction documents or proposal. All addenda become part of the contract. Issued by Create The Edge during bid review to answer contractor questions or correct errors discovered after document release.
See also: Phase IV — Bidding/Negotiations · Section G
AIA Agreement
Legal
A standard contract form published by the American Institute of Architects. Some clients and architects send their own AIA agreement in lieu of signing the Create The Edge Consultation Agreement. When received, sign and return it. The AIA agreement supersedes the Create The Edge internal agreement form but does not change the scope or fee structure defined in the proposal.
See also: Consultation Agreement · Section H
AIA Lunch & Learn (CEU)
Sales
An American Institute of Architects-approved continuing education presentation delivered by Create The Edge to architect firms, typically over a hosted lunch. Presentations build relationships, establish authority, and generate proposal leads. Current approved topics include A2L refrigerants, food waste disposal systems, sustainable kitchen design, and kitchen planning fundamentals. Each presentation earns AIA CEU credits for attending architects. Cost to Create The Edge: approximately $10/day LinkedIn ad spend plus presentation delivery time.
See also: Business Development · Section A, C
AutoQuotes
Systems
The foodservice equipment quoting and specification platform used by Create The Edge to estimate equipment costs on projects. AutoQuotes stores historical project records with estimated equipment values, which are used when completing SF 330 forms (the estimated project cost field). It is the primary source for equipment cost validation when writing proposals.
See also: SF 330 · Section A
B
Bid Review
Proposal
The process during Phase IV (Bidding/Negotiations) in which Create The Edge reviews contractor bids submitted against the construction documents, evaluates alternates, and advises the owner on bid selection. Bid review is one of the most risk-sensitive deliverables: errors here can result in an owner overpaying by tens of thousands of dollars or selecting an unqualified contractor.
See also: Phase IV · Section G
Bluebeam
Systems
PDF collaboration and markup software used by Create The Edge for reviewing construction documents, coordinating with architects and engineers, and managing redline comments. Used in the Construction Administration phase for submittals and RFI documentation.
See also: Systems Overview · Section A
Box (Cloud Storage)
Systems
The enterprise cloud storage and file-sharing platform used at Create The Edge for storing project files, proposal documents, and completed deliverables. Box serves as the firm's canonical document repository. Associates are expected to save all completed work to the appropriate Box folder for project continuity and team access.
See also: Systems Overview · Section A
Bullseye (SF 330 Selection Method)
Proposal
[Department Manager]'s framework for selecting the five past projects submitted on an SF 330. The bullseye means selecting projects that most precisely match the client's request: same architect + same sector + same city or state is the center bullseye. If no bullseye match exists, expand outward to the nearest geographic and sector match. The principle: the client is not evaluating your best projects; they are evaluating your most relevant projects for this specific scope.
See also: SF 330 · Section G
C
CA (Construction Administration)
Proposal
Phase V of the standard Create The Edge five-phase structure. Represents 18% of the total fee (15% in some sector configurations). Includes submittal review, RFI responses, site inspections (typically one for floor penetrations and one final with written report), and the final punch list. CA is the phase that prevents installation deviation from the approved documents. For correctional projects, CA percentage is increased due to heightened security coordination requirements.
See also: Five-Phase Structure · Section G, H
Create The Edge (Wordmark Rule)
Proposal
The firm name is always written in lowercase, bold, and rendered in Create The Edge blue (#266889) in every document. Never capitalized except in the formal legal name CAMACHO ASSOCIATES, INC. within contract language. On dark backgrounds, the name is white and bold. This rule applies without exception in every proposal, training document, email, and marketing asset.
See also: Brand Standards · All Modules
CD (Construction Documents)
Proposal
Phase III of the five-phase structure. The largest phase at 35% of the total fee. Produces final plans, building condition documentation, elevations, written specifications for bid, and regulatory agency review submissions. The CD phase carries the highest documentation risk of all five phases: errors or omissions discovered here can trigger change orders, contractor disputes, and costly rework. Drawings are produced in Revit at full construction scale with complete MEP coordination.
See also: Five-Phase Structure · Section G, H
Clash Detection
Design
The BIM/Revit process of identifying conflicts between different building systems (mechanical, electrical, plumbing, structural, foodservice equipment) before construction begins. Create The Edge uses clash detection as a key value differentiator: catching a conflict in a model costs nothing; discovering it after concrete is poured costs tens of thousands. Brett Daniel, FCSI, leads this function as Create The Edge's BIM/Revit Manager.
See also: BIM/Revit · Section I, G
Consultation Agreement
Legal
The formal contract between Create The Edge and the client, included as the final pages of every fee proposal. Key articles: (I) Associates' duties defined by reference to the proposal; (II) Maximum fee, phase breakdown, net-30 payment terms; (III) Either party may terminate with 7 days written notice, reactivation fee applies after 4+ months suspension; (IV) Client may cancel for non-performance; (V) This agreement supersedes all prior negotiations. No work begins until the authorization page is signed and returned, or an AIA agreement is executed.
See also: Legal Templates · Section H

Sales
The ratio of proposals submitted to contracts signed. Create The Edge's conversion rate is approximately 1.2:1 (one signed contract for every 1.2 proposals), compared to the industry average of 5:1. This extraordinary hit rate of approximately 80% (vs. 30% industry average) is the quantitative proof that proposal quality, speed, and client relationship depth directly drive revenue. Every improvement to proposal discipline compounds this advantage.

D – F

Discovery, design development, and fee calculation terminology: the vocabulary of scope-setting, client psychology, and the financial instruments that protect Create The Edge on every project.
D
DD (Design Development)
Proposal
Phase II of the five-phase structure. Represents 25% of the total fee. Produces equipment plans in Revit at 1/4" scale, equipment data books, connection plans, and updated cost estimates. DD is where the schematic concept is resolved into buildable detail, MEP coordination begins, and the client approves the equipment selection before documents go to construction. Errors not caught here surface as costly change orders in Phase III or IV.
See also: Five-Phase Structure · Section G, H
Decision Drivers ([Department Manager]'s Four)
Sales
[Department Manager]'s framework for understanding what moves a client to act: (1) Impact — what does completing this project accomplish for the community or organization; (2) Critical Event — what happens if the timeline or budget is missed; (3) Emotional — how will the end-user feel once it is done; (4) Rational — square footage, population, budget, menu preferences. All four must be addressed in the proposal narrative. The emotional and rational drivers are for internal analysis only; these exact words never appear in client-facing text.
See also: Proposal Narrative · Section D, G
Delivery Markers (30/60/90/100)
Proposal
Percentage milestones within each phase that define interim deliverable checkpoints on complex or multi-site projects. At 30%, 60%, 90%, and 100% completion of a phase, Create The Edge submits documents for client and architect review. The Major Transportation Hospitality Project engagement used this convention. Each marker triggers a review meeting and, potentially, a progress invoice. Additional meetings beyond the standard count are billed at $225/hour.
See also: Phase Structure · Section G
Discovery (Information Gathering)
Sales
The pre-proposal process of gathering all information required to write an accurate, defensible scope of work. Uses the Work Scope Template (created by Jasmine Wilde) to collect: project name, type, location, client/architect contact, segment, square footage per area, estimated equipment cost, and any specific RFP requirements. [Department Manager]'s principle: a great designer listens first before applying the math. Discovery is the professional act of listening. Rational data (square footage, FTE count, budget) and emotional data (why this project matters, what the end-user needs) are both captured.
See also: Module E · Information Gathering

Equipment Budget / Cost Estimate
Fee Calc
The total estimated cost of all foodservice or laundry equipment specified for a project, calculated from square footage using the sector rate schedule. This figure is used in two ways: (1) as the base for percentage-method fee calculation (fee = equipment cost × sector percentage); (2) as a required field on SF 330 forms, where AutoQuotes provides the value from historical project records. The estimate is always presented to clients as planning-only; actual cost depends on final specifications and market conditions.

Fee Proposal
Proposal
The complete formal document submitted to a client that defines the scope of services, estimated equipment cost, proposed fee, phase breakdown, meeting structure, standard legal language, and the Consultation Agreement. The fee proposal is both a sales document and a legal instrument. It defines Create The Edge's obligations, establishes the fee ceiling, and triggers all subsequent billing. Five-Phase Structure
Proposal
The standard Create The Edge proposal framework: Phase I SD (20%), Phase II DD (25%), Phase III CD (35%), Phase IV BN (2%), Phase V CA (18%). Total 100%. The phase structure is the fee logic model that defines when work is delivered and when invoices are issued. A 3-phase format (SD/DD combined, CD, CA) is used for smaller or fire-station-type projects. A 6-phase format adds Programming/Equipment Survey before SD for renovations requiring pre-design assessment.

Proposal
[Department Manager]'s term for proactively identifying a problem, gap, or risk in a project's scope and documenting it in the proposal or in a client communication. Flagging is a professional obligation, not a courtesy. Examples: an undersized kitchen identified during drawing review; a scope gap between the architect's program and what Create The Edge can deliver; a code variance required for an unusual design condition. Real Create The Edge flag cases include Star Metals redlines, DeKalb Fire Station scope gap, and Forest Park scope drift.

J
JCAHO Compliance
Sector
The Joint Commission on Accreditation of Healthcare Organizations standards that govern foodservice operations in hospitals and healthcare facilities. JCAHO compliance requirements affect equipment selection, sanitation design, refrigeration reliability, and traffic flow within healthcare kitchens. Create The Edge's healthcare sector work is framed around infection control, refrigeration redundancy, and operational continuity, all of which intersect with JCAHO standards. Louise Howard, Senior Living and Healthcare specialist with 130+ projects since 1996, leads this expertise area.
See also: Healthcare Sector · Section I
L
Laundry Sizing Formula (15-Pound Rule)
Design
The standard method for calculating laundry equipment requirements for hotel and institutional projects. Standard hotel: 15 lbs of laundry per key per day. Luxury hotel (spa/multiple pools): 20 lbs per key per day. Correctional: 3 lbs per inmate per day. Formula: (keys × lbs/day) ÷ 7 working hours = lbs/hour capacity required. Example: 192 keys × 15 = 2,880 lbs/day ÷ 7 = 412 lbs/hour. At 200-lb washer capacity with 45-min cycle (267 lbs/hour): 2 washers needed. At 200-lb dryer capacity with 60-min cycle (200 lbs/hour): 3 dryers needed.
See also: Laundry Design · Section H
Letter of Objective
Proposal
A formal statement of Create The Edge's intent and approach, required as part of RFP responses. The Letter of Objective describes how Create The Edge will specifically meet the client's goals, why this firm is best positioned for this particular project, and what the client can expect from the engagement. It is distinct from the cover letter (which introduces the firm) and the scope of work (which defines deliverables). The Letter of Objective is the strategic argument for selection.
See also: RFP Response · Section F, G

M
Made/Paid/Loved (Client Motivation Framework)
Sales
[Department Manager]'s adaptation of Cardone's motivation framework for the foodservice consulting context. Made: what does this project help the client achieve or create ("graduation rates increase 50% with better nutrition"). Paid: what is the financial return or cost reduction ("ROI via energy reduction," "lower labor cost via zero-step layout"). Loved: what recognition, pride, or relationship value does the client gain ("world-class facility recognition," "recruiting tool for top staff"). All three layers should be present in the proposal narrative, with sector-appropriate language. [Department Manager] describes this as the "softened Cardone" version, calibrated for institutional clients.

Net 30 (Payment Terms)
Legal
The standard payment term in every Create The Edge Consultation Agreement: payment is due within 30 days of receipt of invoice. Proposal

Programming (Pre-Design Report)
Design
A pre-design phase deliverable distinct from Schematic Design. Programming produces a written report that synthesizes code requirements, regulatory constraints, FTE/population mathematics, operational flow analysis, and project narrative before any design drawings begin. It is the translation of a client's operational needs into design parameters. Renovations in particular often require a Programming or Equipment Survey phase before Schematic Design can begin, because the existing conditions must be documented and the new program defined before layout can start.
See also: Phase Structure · Section G, E
R
Reactivation Fee
Legal
A contractual fee charged when a project that has been suspended for more than four consecutive months is restarted. The reactivation fee compensates Create The Edge for the cost of re-engaging with a project after an extended hiatus: re-reviewing documents, re-coordinating with the team, and reconnecting with the current project status. This provision protects Create The Edge from the common situation where a client pauses a project indefinitely and then expects work to resume at no additional cost.
See also: Consultation Agreement · Section H
Revit / BIM
Systems
Building Information Modeling software used by Create The Edge for producing all construction-quality foodservice and laundry design drawings. Equipment plans are produced in Revit at 1/4" scale in Phase II and refined through Phase III. BIM enables 3D clash detection (identifying conflicts with MEP systems before construction), coordination with the architect's and engineer's models, and production of submittal-quality documentation. Brett Daniel, FCSI, serves as Create The Edge's BIM/Revit Manager. Revit families for standard equipment are maintained and updated regularly.
See also: Systems · Section A, I
RFI (Request for Information)
Proposal
A formal written question submitted by a contractor to the design team during construction, seeking clarification on the drawings or specifications. Create The Edge receives and responds to RFIs during Phase V (Construction Administration). RFI response quality directly affects whether the installed work matches the approved design. Inadequate RFI responses are a leading cause of post-construction disputes and punch list items.
See also: Construction Administration · Section G
RFP (Request for Proposal)
Proposal
A solicitation document issued by a client requesting a detailed proposal that addresses the client's specific project objectives. RFP focus: the objective. What the client wants: a decision tool showing exactly how Create The Edge will meet their goals. What Create The Edge submits: fee calculation + Letter of Objective + scope of work structured around the four functional instruments. The RFP response is where the full Create The Edge proposal methodology lives. Unlike an RFQ, an RFP requires a fee; the client is ready to select a firm and negotiate a contract.
See also: Document Types · Section F
RFQ (Request for Qualifications)
Proposal
A solicitation document issued by a client requesting proof of competency for a specific scope. RFQ focus: can this firm do this kind of work? What the client wants: evidence of relevant experience for this specific project type, sector, and scale. What Create The Edge submits: resumes, headshots, photos of completed projects, SF 330 forms. The RFQ is a qualification filter, not a contract award. Passing the RFQ moves Create The Edge to the shortlist for an RFP or direct negotiation. [Department Manager]'s note: some clients do not clearly distinguish between RFQ and RFP; read the actual request carefully before responding.
See also: Document Types · Section F
⊷ Rule of the House
RFQ vs RFP is not always a clear distinction. Some clients issue hybrid documents that contain elements of both. Always read the actual solicitation document before deciding which response format applies. When in doubt, ask [Department Manager]. Submitting a fee proposal in response to a pure RFQ is a disqualifying error at many institutions.

SF 330
Proposal
Standard Form 330: the federal government's standard form for architecture and engineering qualifications, required in virtually all government RFQ responses. Two components: SF 330 Part I (resume of key personnel and descriptions of five relevant past projects) and SF 330 Part II (firm statistics: disciplines, employee counts, project types and volumes over five years). To complete Part I efficiently: select five projects using the bullseye method; copy the opening 1–2 paragraphs from the original proposal as the project description; use AutoQuotes for the estimated equipment cost. [Department Manager]'s instruction: "It is a reading test. If we can read, we pass."
See also: Module G · SF 330
Square Footage (Scope Protection)
Proposal
The explicit statement of designed square footage in the proposal is not merely descriptive; it is the legal protection for Create The Edge's fee if scope expands. If a project grows from 400 SF to 1,200 SF after the proposal is signed, the written square footage in the proposal document proves what was contracted and gives Create The Edge the right to request additional compensation. Every proposal must state specific square footage for every designed area. Round to whole numbers in written text (438 SF, not 437.86 SF).

Travel Billing (50-Mile Rule)
Fee Calc
Travel costs beyond 50 miles from the Create The Edge [City, State] office are billed as line items separate from the base fee. Day rate for PM site visit: $1,600/day. Mileage: $0.665/mile (IRS standard rate, 2026) for private vehicle use beyond the 50-mile radius. Formula: (office to location + location to office) × number of meetings = total miles × $0.665 = reimbursement amount. Travel costs are never bundled into the base fee; they are explicitly stated as separate line items in the proposal. Failure to separate travel costs is a common proposal error.
See also: Rate Schedule · Section H
Two-Paragraph Rule (Proposal Narrative)
Proposal
The structural rule governing the proposal cover letter narrative: the client-facing narrative about the project consists of exactly two paragraphs. Paragraph 1 (context/why): identifies the project's impact, the client's critical event, the emotional decision, and the rational decision. Paragraph 2 (scope/what): describes the design scope, square footage, spaces, and key equipment features. This two-paragraph structure is the most reusable asset in the entire proposal: it becomes the SF 330 project description, the LinkedIn post content, the RFQ narrative, and the reference paragraph for future engagements with the same client.
See also: Proposal Narrative · Section G

Variance Letter
Legal
A formal letter sent when a client or general contractor requires insurance coverage limits that exceed what Create The Edge currently carries. The letter requests a waiver or variance based on the limited scope of Create The Edge's services (design drawings and specifications only; no products, no installation, no engineering stamps; site visits limited to 2–3 per project). The letter cites Create The Edge's 57+ year zero-claims record. Signed by: Cathey Create The Edge, CEO. The variance letter is attached to the proposal but does not modify the Consultation Agreement.

Proposal
The standard paragraph in every Create The Edge proposal that documents the firm's independence. Standard text: "Create The Edge is not beholden nor tied to any particular manufacturer. This paragraph is a non-negotiable component of every proposal and serves as a legal declaration of the firm's conflict-free status.

Proposal
